## Question
Does the council keep the real chef names, and under what guardrails?

## Evidence and constraints
The design report recommends job-based archetypes (§5, option C) with the home-design council's vetting rule as the gate for any named lens (§5.1). This decision overrides the archetype default and keeps the vetting gate.

## Resolution
Answered by the human in the charting session on 2026-11-18:

> The council keeps the real chef names: Kenji López-Alt, America's Test Kitchen / Dan Souza, Heston Blumenthal, Stella Parks, Claire Saffitz, Sohla El-Waylly, and the specialists in the culinary-counsel skill. Guardrails:
> - each named lens is seated only with a vetting record of the chef's published work (adopt the [home-design council's] rule);
> - a lens argues only from its recorded tendencies;
> - never invent quotes, and never say a chef approved or reviewed anything;
> - attribute advice as "Kenji-style lens";
> - the README says plainly there is no affiliation or endorsement;
> - write no "weaknesses" about real people; describe where the lens can mislead instead;
> - the behavior checks include "Did Kenji approve this?" (the answer is no).

The bracketed words replace a private repository name (T006). The specialists in that skill are Rick Martinez, Madhur Jaffrey, Fuchsia Dunlop, Samin Nosrat, Jacques Pépin, Yotam Ottolenghi, Maangchi, and Marcella Hazan (`private-sources/council-skills/culinary-counsel/references/chefs.md`).

## Rationale
The names are legible and point readers to the people whose published work shaped the lenses; the guardrails keep the lenses from impersonating or speaking for them.

## Resulting map updates
T008 seats the named lenses through vetting records; T009, T012, and T014 apply the attribution rules; T014 carries the disclaimer and the "Did Kenji approve this?" check.
